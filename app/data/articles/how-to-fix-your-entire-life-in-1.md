# How to Change Your Entire Life in One Day

### Do this before 2026

*By DAN KOE | December 23, 2025*

---

You're probably going to abandon your New Year's resolution.

That's okay. Most people do (research shows failure rates as high as 80-90%), because most people don't actually want to change deep down. They want to change their life the wrong way. They make New Year's resolutions because everyone else does—we're wired to impress others more than impress ourselves... we construct shallow meaning from identity games—but it doesn't meet the requirement for real change, which is far deeper than convincing yourself to be more disciplined and productive this year.

I'm not here to lecture you. I've abandoned ten times more goals than I've set. I think that's true for most people. But the fact that people try to change their lives and fail almost every time is valid. It's even become a meme—gyms are packed in January, back to normal by February.

However, while I think New Year's resolutions are silly, it's always wise to reflect on the life you hate and push yourself toward a better direction—and we'll discuss that.

Human nature is a bastard, and the worst feeling is making a promise to yourself that you can't help but break. You start feeling helpless, and if you don't know what you're supposed to do, you might continue this cycle for years: always wanting to change, never able to.

So, whether you want to start a business, get in shape, or move toward a more meaningful life without quitting in two weeks, I want to share 7 perspectives on behavior change, psychology, and productivity that you've probably never heard before, so you can actually do it in 2026.

This will be comprehensive content.

Not the kind you read and forget.

This is something you save, take notes on, and carve out time to think about.

The final protocol—going deep into your psychology and excavating the life you truly want—takes a full day to complete, but the effects last far longer than that.

All I ask is your full attention. Jump to the next section if you're bored, come back later to fill in the blanks if you need to.

Let's begin.

## 1. You're Not Where You Want To Be Because You're Not Yet The Person Who Gets There

When it comes to New Year's resolutions, people focus on only one of two requirements for success:

1. Change your actions to move toward a goal (least important, second-order)
2. Change who you are so your behaviors follow naturally (most important, first-order)

Most people set a surface-level goal, stay disciplined for the first few weeks with motivation, then revert to old ways effortlessly because they're trying to build a good life on a rotten foundation.

If this doesn't sound right, let's walk through an example.

Think of a successful person. It could be a fit fitness enthusiast, a billionaire founder/CEO, or a charismatic person who can chat with a group of people without anxiety.

Do you think that fitness person needs to "try hard" to eat healthy? Does the CEO need to force themselves to show up and lead the team? It might seem that way from the surface, but the truth is *they can't imagine living any other way.* The fitness person has to try hard to eat **unhealthy**. The CEO has to force themselves to sleep later than their alarm—they hate every second of it.

To some people, my lifestyle looks extreme and disciplined. But to me, it's natural, and I say that not to compare to any other way of living. I just like living this way. When my mom tells me I should take a break, go out, have fun... I refrain from telling her: "Why would I do what I'm currently doing if I don't find it fun?"

Listen to this next sentence carefully.

If you want a specific life outcome, you must have the lifestyle that creates that outcome long before you reach it.

If someone says they want to lose 30 pounds, I usually don't believe them. Not because I think they can't do it, but because often the same people say "once I lose the weight I'll be able to start enjoying life again." I hate to tell you, but if you don't adopt the lifestyle that makes you lose that weight—forever—and find a *reason* with more gravity than your previous lifestyle, you'll go right back to where you started, unhappily, and you can say you wasted resources you can never get back: time.

When you genuinely change who you are, all those habits that don't push you toward your goals become disgusting because you're deeply and clearly aware of what those behaviors accumulate into. You're satisfied with your current standards because you aren't fully aware of what they are, or where they lead. We'll discuss how to find this out, but we need to take it step by step.

You say you want to change. You say you want "financial freedom" and "to be healthy," but your behavior shows the opposite—and there's a reason. And it's much deeper than you think.

## 2. You're Not Where You Want To Be Because You Don't Genuinely "Want" To Get There

> Trust only in action. Life happens at the level of events, not words. Trust in action.
> —Alfred Adler

If you want to change who you are, you must understand how the mind works so you can start reprogramming it.

The first step in understanding the mind is understanding that all behavior is goal-oriented. When you think about it, it's actually obvious, but when we go deep, most people don't want to hear this.

You take a step forward because you want to get somewhere.

You scratch your nose because you want the itchy feeling to go away.

These are clear, but most of the time, your goals are unconscious. You might not be aware that when you're sitting on the couch in the middle of the day, you're killing time until your next responsibility comes—a simple example.

At a more unconscious, complex level, the goals you're pursuing might hurt you, but you rationalize your behavior in a socially acceptable way so you don't look like a failure.

For example, if you can't stop procrastinating on work, you might explain it with "lack of discipline," but you're really trying to achieve a goal like usual. In this case, that goal might be protecting yourself from the judgment that comes with completing and sharing your work.

If you say you want to quit your dead-end job but have no real reason to keep doing it, you might start thinking you don't have enough courage, or you were never a "risk-taker," but the truth is you're pursuing safety, predictable goals, and an excuse to not look like a failure to people who are also doing jobs with no future.

The lesson here is that real change requires changing your goals.

I don't mean setting some shallow goals, because doing that serves an unconscious goal that's actually hurting you—there's been plenty of discussion about this in the productivity field. I'm talking about changing your perspective. Because that's what goals are. Goals are a projection of the future that acts as a perceptual lens, making you notice information, ideas, and resources that help achieve that goal.

Now let's go deeper, because if you don't understand this, the next parts will be increasingly hard to escape.

## 3. You're Not Where You Want To Be Because You're Afraid To Get There

> The important thing to remember is how you got the idea or where it came from is not important at all. You may never have met a professional hypnotist. You may never have been formally hypnotized. But if you accepted an idea—from yourself, your teacher, your parents, friends, advertising, from any other source—and further, if you firmly believe that idea is true, it has the same power over you as the hypnotist's words have over the subject.
> —Maxwell Maltz

Here's how you became who you are today, and how you'll become who you'll be tomorrow. The anatomy of identity:

1. You want to achieve a goal
2. You perceive reality through that goal's lens
3. You only notice "important" information and ideas that help you achieve that goal (learning)
4. You act toward that goal and receive feedback that you're making progress
5. You repeat that behavior until it becomes automatic and unconscious (conditioning)
6. That behavior becomes part of who you think you are ("I'm the kind of person who...")
7. You maintain your identity to stay psychologically consistent
8. Your identity shapes new goals, restarting the cycle, and if that identity doesn't serve a good life, things get bad fast

The unfortunate truth is you have to break this cycle between steps 6 and 7, but the process started in your childhood.

You had a goal to survive.

You depended on your parents to teach you how to survive. You had to conform. Because most people are raised through rewards and punishments, you get punished unless you adopt their beliefs and values. You don't truly think for yourself until you see through this.

But your parents went through this process too, throughout their lives. That's where it gets dangerous. Your parents, unless they broke the pattern themselves, were shaped by industrial-era ideas of success. They also carry the best and worst conditioning from their parents and grandparents.

Go another layer deeper, and once you satisfied your body's survival needs (which is pretty easy to do in today's world, you're almost born into safety), you start surviving at a conceptual or ideological level. You might not be trying to protect and reproduce your body, but you absolutely protect and reproduce your mind. It's not hard to see internet culture wars, where participants are individuals and groups fighting for identity.

When your body feels threatened, you go into fight or flight.

When your identity feels threatened, the same thing happens.

If you heavily identify with a political ideology through the process we just discussed, you feel threatened when someone challenges your beliefs. You really feel the stress. Emotionally, you feel like you've been slapped. Because most people don't analyze their emotions for truth, you tend to fall into information bubbles and double down on views that hurt you and others.

If you grew up in a religious family and didn't think for yourself, you attack people who threaten those within your psychological safe zone.

The same thing happens when you unconsciously see yourself as a lawyer, gamer, or someone else who doesn't take action toward a better life.

## 4. The Life You Want Exists at a Specific Mind Layer

The mind develops through predictable stages over time.

When you're born, you're like a little survival sponge, absorbing whatever beliefs you can get (mostly determined by your culture) so you can feel safe and secure. If you're not careful, your mind can solidify, making it hard to live a meaningful life.

This is well-documented in models like Maslow's hierarchy of needs, Greuter's self-development stages, and Spiral Dynamics, each building on the previous, but it's not hard to observe in society.

I've talked about these multiple times and synthesized them into my own Human 3.0 model, but here's the 80/20 of 9 self-development stages as a review:

**9 Stages of Self-Development:**

1. **Impulsive**—no separation between impulse and behavior. Black-and-white thinking. Example: A toddler hits when angry because feeling and action are the same thing.

2. **Self-protective**—the world is dangerous, you learn to take care of yourself. Example: A child learns to hide report cards, lie to get out of chores, figure out what adults want to hear.

3. **Conformist**—you are your group and its rules feel like reality itself. Example: Someone genuinely cannot understand why someone would vote differently than their family or group.

4. **Self-aware**—you notice you have an inner life that doesn't match your outer appearance. Example: Sitting in church realizing you're not sure you believe what everyone else seems to believe, but not knowing how to process that feeling yet.

5. **Conscientious**—you build your own principles and hold yourself accountable. Example: Leaving a family religion after careful study and adopting a personal philosophy you can defend, or making a career plan with clear milestones because you believe the right effort produces the right results.

6. **Individualist**—you see your principles are shaped by context and begin holding them more loosely. Example: Realizing your political views have more to do with where you grew up than objective truth, or noticing your ambitious career goals are really about getting validation from your father.

7. **Strategist**—you work with systems while being aware of your participation in them. Example: Leading an organization while actively questioning your blind spots, or participating in politics knowing your perspective is partial and shaped by biases you can't fully see.

8. **Construct-aware**—you see all frameworks, including your identity, as useful fictions. Example: Holding your spiritual beliefs metaphorically rather than literally, knowing the map is not the territory, or watching yourself play the role of "founder" or "thought leader" with mild amusement.

9. **Integrative**—the separation between self and life dissolves. Example: Work, rest, and play feel like the same thing. No one needing to become anything, just presence responding to what arises.

For most people reading this, I think you'd believe you're somewhere between 4 and 8, which is a huge range. Those closer to 8 read these for learning or entertainment. Those closer to 4 are genuinely looking for change. You feel destined for something big, but you can't understand everything yet because clearly there's a lot at play.

The good news is where you are doesn't matter that much, because moving through any stage follows a pattern.

## 5. Intelligence Is The Ability To Get What You Want From Life

> The only real test of intelligence is whether you get what you want out of life.
> —Naval Ravikant

There's a formula for success.

One component is **agency**.

One component is **opportunity** (many like to misidentify this as "privilege"—because they don't have the other components).

The final component is **intelligence**.

If you have high agency but low opportunity, no matter how likely you are to act toward a goal, it doesn't matter because it's not a goal that will produce much fruit.

If you have opportunity and agency but lack intelligence, you'll never fully benefit from that opportunity.

First, we've talked about agency here before. In terms of opportunity, I can't tell you to change your physical location, but if you can't see the abundance of digital opportunities right in front of you, I don't know what to tell you.

That said, I want to focus on what intelligence is in the context of these two other components and this letter.

Cybernetics comes from the Greek *kybernetikos*, meaning "to steer" or "to be good at steering." It's also known as "the art of getting what you want."

So, if Naval's definition of intelligence is getting what you want from life, understanding cybernetics helps you get there faster.

Cybernetics describes the properties of intelligent systems:

* There's a goal.
* Act toward that goal.
* Perceive where you are.
* Compare it to the goal.
* Act again based on feedback.

You can judge intelligence by how well a system can iterate and persist through trial and error.

A ship blown off course corrects direction toward its destination. A thermostat senses temperature changes and flips a switch. The pancreas secretes insulin after blood sugar spikes.

What does this have to do with getting what you want from life?

Everything.

Acting, perceiving, comparing, and understanding systems from a meta-perspective is the foundation of high intelligence.

High intelligence is the ability to iterate, persist, and understand the big picture. **The hallmark of low intelligence is the inability to learn from mistakes.**

Low intelligence people get stuck on problems rather than solving them. They encounter obstacles and quit. Like a writer who gives up on building an audience because they lack the ability to try new things, experiment, and figure out what works for them.

High intelligence is realizing that any problem can be solved on a large enough time scale. The reality is, you can achieve any goal you set your mind to. This is undisprovable within reasonable bounds.

Intelligence is realizing there exists a set of choices you can make that leads to achieving the goal you want. You understand that ideas are layered, and you can't jump from a draft to Google Docs in one step. Even if that goal is currently impossible, you just don't have the resources—those resources might be invented in the next few years—to accomplish that thing.

When I talk about "goals," as I'll continue to repeat, I'm not talking from the typical self-help perspective, even though that's a useful lens to sometimes adopt.

I'm talking from a teleological or Greek *kosmos* perspective—everything has *purpose*. Everything is part of a larger whole.

Goals determine how you see the world.

Goals determine what's "success" or "failure."

You can try to "enjoy the journey," but if you're pursuing the wrong goal, you won't enjoy it.

Your mind is the operating system of reality.

That system is composed of goals.

For most people, those goals are assigned to them. Programmed like lines of code in your psychology.

*Get an education. Get a job. Get offended. Play the victim. Retire at 65.*

A known path that doesn't work.

To become more intelligent, you must:

* Reject the known path
* Dive into the unknown
* Set new, higher goals that expand your mind
* Embrace chaos and allow growth
* Learn universal principles of nature
* Become a deep generalist

This leads us perfectly into the next section.

## 6. How To Launch An Entirely New Life In One Day

> The best periods in my life were always the ones that came right after I felt absolutely bored with what I had achieved.

How do you go deep inside yourself?

How do you become aware of your conditioning?

How do you get life-changing insights and truths?

Through the simple, but usually painful, act of asking questions.

Few people do this, and you can tell by the way they talk about or opine on any topic. Asking questions is thinking, and few people truly do it.

I want to give you a comprehensive protocol you can use annually to reset your life and launch a highly productive season. This protocol helps you ask the right questions.

These questions will cover from macro to micro: where you want to go, what you need to do to get there, and what you can do immediately to start pushing the needle toward that reality.

It takes a full day to complete, so I recommend following the protocol exactly. You'll need a pen, paper, and an open mind.

When I observe patterns of people who successfully flip their identity, it happens right after tension builds. Specifically, I notice people tend to go through 3 stages.

1. **Dissonance**—they feel like they don't belong in their current life and are sufficiently bored with lack of progress.
2. **Uncertainty**—they don't know what comes next, so they either experiment or get lost and feel worse.
3. **Discovery**—they discover what they want to pursue, making 6 years of progress in 6 months.

So, the purpose of this protocol is to help you reach the dissonance point, navigate through uncertainty, and discover what you genuinely want to achieve, so profoundly that the clarity is overwhelming and distractions lose weight.

The structure of this protocol allows it to be completed in one day. In the morning, you do psychological excavation to discover your hidden motivations. During the day, you're prompted with interruptions to keep you out of autopilot mode and thinking about your life. At night, you synthesize insights into a direction you start moving tomorrow.

I can't guarantee this works for everyone because I can't guarantee everyone reading this is at the right point in their own story for these perspectives to make an impact. You can't put the climax at the beginning of a book and expect it to be interesting.

### Part 1) Morning—Psychological Excavation—Vision and Anti-Vision

First we must create a new framework, or perceptual lens, for your mind to operate from.

It's like creating a new shell, leaving the old one, and slowly growing into it over time. It's normal for it to feel uncomfortable at first. That's good.

Carve out 15-30 minutes (length of a YouTube video... you can do it) to think about and answer these questions. Don't try to outsource this thinking to AI. I want you to break through the limiters on your mind. If you can't answer these questions immediately, come back later.

1. What's that low-grade constant dissatisfaction you've learned to tolerate? Not deep suffering, but what you've learned to tolerate. (If you didn't hate it, you'd tolerate it)

2. What do you repeatedly complain about but never actually change? Write down the top three complaints you've expressed in the past year.

3. For each complaint: what would someone observing your behavior (not your words) conclude you actually want?

4. What would be unbearable to admit to someone you deeply respect about your current life?

These questions are meant to make you aware of the pain in your current life. Now, we need to turn these into what I call an "anti-vision," which is a brutal recognition of the life you don't want. This way, you can channel that negative energy toward positive direction and act from intrinsic motivation.

5. If everything stayed exactly the same for the next five years, describe an ordinary Tuesday. Where do you wake up? How does your body feel? What's the first thing you think about? Who's around you? What do you do between 9am and 6pm? How do you feel at 10pm?

6. Now imagine ten years from now. What did you miss? What opportunities closed? Who gave up on you? What do people say about you when you're not in the room?

7. You're at the end of your life. You lived the safe version. You never broke that pattern. What was the cost? What did you never allow yourself to feel, try, or become?

8. Who in your life is already living the future you just described? Who's on the same path but five, ten, twenty years ahead? How do you feel when you think about becoming them?

9. What identity must you truly abandon to change? ("I'm the kind of person who...") What would it socially cost to no longer be that person?

10. What's the most embarrassing reason you haven't changed? The one that makes you sound weak, afraid, or lazy rather than reasonable?

11. If your current behavior is a form of self-protection, what exactly are you protecting? And what's that protection costing you?

If you answered these questions truthfully, and you're at the right chapter in your life, you'll feel a profound unease about your current lifestyle, possibly even disgust. Now, we need to channel that energy toward positive direction. We need to create a minimum viable vision, because your vision is like a product. It's not clear at first, but becomes stronger and more effective over time and experience.

12. Forget feasibility first. If you could snap your fingers and live a different life in three years, not what's realistic, but what do you truly want? What does an ordinary Tuesday look like? Same level of detail as question 5.

13. What must you believe about yourself to make that life feel natural rather than forced? Write an identity statement: "I'm the kind of person who..."

14. If you were already that person, what's one thing you'd do this week?

Answer all these questions first thing tomorrow.

### Part 2) All Day—Breaking Autopilot—Disrupting Unconscious Patterns

These journal exercises are cute, but we want real change.

If you don't break the unconscious patterns that are keeping you the same, it won't happen.

All day, I want you to think about everything you wrote in part one. Beyond that, I don't want you to forget to think. Take this seriously. You won't change by doing the same things for the rest of your life. You need to consciously force a pattern break.

Now take time to create reminders or calendar events in your phone. Include the questions in the reminders so you can start thinking about them immediately.

The more random and less conflicting with your schedule they are, the better.

* **11:00 AM:** What am I avoiding by doing what I'm currently doing?
* **1:30 PM:** If someone filmed the last two hours, what would they conclude I want from life?
* **3:15 PM:** Am I moving toward a life I hate or a life I want?
* **5:00 PM:** What am I pretending doesn't matter but actually matters most?
* **7:30 PM:** What did I do today from identity protection rather than genuine desire? (Hint: most of what you do is)
* **9:00 PM:** When did I feel most alive today? When did I feel most dead?

To make it worse, schedule these for when you have commutes, walks, or lying in bed.

* If I no longer needed people to see me as [the identity you wrote in question 10], what would change?
* Where in my life did I trade energy for safety?
* What's the minimum version of the person I want to become that I could be tomorrow?

### Part 3) Evening—Synthesizing Insights—Entering A Productive Season

If you followed this process, I'd be surprised if you didn't have at least one potentially life-changing insight by the end. Now, we need to make these known, integrate them into who we are, and act on them to start consolidating our journey toward a new mind layer.

15. After today, what feels most true about why you've been stuck?

16. What's the real enemy? Name it clearly. Not circumstances. Not other people. The internal pattern or belief that's been running the show.

17. Write one sentence summarizing the life you refuse to let yours become. This is your compressed anti-vision. Reading it should give you feelings.

18. Write one sentence summarizing what you're moving toward, knowing it will evolve. This is your minimum viable vision.

Finally, we need to create goals.

19. **One-Year Lens:** What must be true a year from now to know you've broken the old pattern? One specific thing.

20. **One-Month Lens:** What must be true a month from now for the one-year lens to stay possible?

21. **Daily Lens:** What 2-3 actions can you time-block tomorrow that the person you're becoming would simply do?

There's a lot here. Hopefully it's helpful. But we have one final piece to lock it all in.

## 7. Turn Your Life Into A Video Game

> The optimal state of inner experience is order in consciousness. This happens when psychological energy—or attention—is invested in realistic goals, and when skills and opportunities for action are matched to those goals. Pursuing a goal brings order to consciousness because one must concentrate attention on the task at hand and temporarily forget everything else.
> —Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi

You now have all the components for a good life.

It might help to organize all your insights into a coherent plan. Get a new piece of paper and write these 6 components:

* **Anti-Vision**—what's your pet peeve, or the life you never want to experience again?
* **Vision**—what life do you consider ideal, that could improve as you work toward it?
* **One-Year Goal**—what will your life look like a year from now, and is it closer to what you want?
* **One-Month Project**—what do you need to learn? What skills do you need to acquire? What can you build that pushes you toward the one-year goal?
* **Daily Leverage**—what are the priority, project-driving tasks that can move my project closer to completion?
* **Constraints**—what am I unwilling to sacrifice to achieve my vision from scratch?

Why is this so powerful?

Because these components actually create your own little world. If you're meant to pursue this level of goal at this stage of your life, you'll have no choice but to become obsessed. You'll feel pulled by something greater. You won't see anything else as an option.

You turn your life into a video game.

Because games are the epitome of obsession, enjoyment, and flow states. They have all the components that lead to focus and clarity, so if we reverse-engineer what those components are, we can live in states of deeper enjoyment, less distraction, and more success.

Your vision is how you **win**. At least until the game evolves.

Your anti-vision is what **danger** looks like. What happens if you lose or quit.

Your one-year goal is the **mission**. The only priority in your life.

Your one-year project is the **boss fight**. How you gain experience and loot.

Your daily leverage is the **quest**. The daily process that unlocks new opportunities.

Your constraints are the **rules**. The limitations that encourage creativity.

All these are like concentric circles, like a force field, protecting your mind from distraction and temptation.

The more you play the game, the stronger this force field becomes, and soon it becomes who you are, and you wouldn't want it any other way.
